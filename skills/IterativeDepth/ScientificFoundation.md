# Iterative Depth — Scientific Foundation

> Returning to the same problem through multiple structured viewpoints to extract deeper understanding ranks among the most broadly validated moves in serious inquiry.

## The Pattern Beneath the Name

"Iterative Depth" names no single technique — it labels a **meta-pattern** that cognitive scientists, AI researchers, requirements engineers, designers, and philosophers each discovered on their own. Independent convergence across that many disciplines is itself evidence the pattern is real.

The pattern, stated plainly: **re-examining one phenomenon N times, each time from a systematically different viewpoint, yields understanding unreachable by any single examination.**

---

## Where the Pattern Shows Up

### Cognitive Science & Epistemology

**1. Hermeneutic Circle** (Hans-Georg Gadamer, 1960)
Understanding grows by cycling between parts and whole. Each reading of a text revises pre-understanding, which changes what the next reading discloses. Understanding is "always on the way" — never finished in one pass.
- *Source: Truth and Method (1960); Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*
- *Carries over as: each pass revises the "pre-understanding" of what the user wants*

**2. Triangulation** (Norman Denzin, 1970)
Study one phenomenon through several methods, investigators, theories, or data sources. Four forms: method, investigator, theory, and data-source triangulation. Cures single-method bias.
- *Source: The Research Act (1970); PMC: Principles, Scope, and Limitations of Methodological Triangulation*
- *Carries over as: each pass IS a distinct "method" trained on the same problem*

**3. Cognitive Flexibility Theory** (Rand Spiro et al., 1988)
Revisit the same material from several perspectives at different times to build transferable, deep understanding. "Criss-crossing the landscape" of a concept from multiple directions.
- *Source: Cognition and Instruction, 1988*
- *Carries over as: each lens is one more "crossing" of the problem landscape*

**4. Dialectical Thinking** (Hegel, refined by many)
Thesis–antithesis–synthesis cycles. Confront a proposition with its negation, then integrate both into a fuller understanding. Every cycle deepens the analysis.
- *Carries over as: constraint inversion and failure lenses as organized antithesis*

**5. Reflective Equilibrium** (John Rawls, 1971; Nelson Goodman, 1955)
Justified belief comes from shuttling between particular judgments and general principles, revising each against the other until coherence holds. Iterative by design, with no fixed stopping point.
- *Source: A Theory of Justice (1971); Fact, Fiction, and Forecast (1955); Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*
- *Carries over as: each pass tests the emerging picture against a fresh angle, revising until the ISC model coheres*

**6. Equilibration** (Jean Piaget, 1975)
Minds grow through disequilibrium–equilibrium cycles. Information that contradicts existing schemas forces accommodation — the restructuring of mental models. This cycle IS the engine of cognitive growth.
- *Source: The Equilibration of Cognitive Structures (1975)*
- *Carries over as: each new lens manufactures productive disequilibrium that pushes genuine understanding forward*

**7. Abductive Reasoning** (Charles Sanders Peirce, 1903)
Propose hypotheses for surprising observations, then iteratively test and revise them. Peirce held this provisional by nature. Different outputs from one prompt are different abductive hypotheses.
- *Source: Harvard Lectures on Pragmatism (1903); Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy*
- *Carries over as: non-determinism in AI is abductive hypothesis generation, not noise — a FEATURE, not a defect*

**8. Progressive Refinement of Mental Models** (Gentner & Stevens, 1983)
Mental models mature through iterative elaboration: each pass adds detail, fixes errors, folds in new information. Early passes sketch rough structure; later passes add precision and catch edge cases.
- *Source: Mental Models (1983); Johnson-Laird, Mental Models (1983)*
- *Carries over as: the cognitive machinery behind WHY successive lens passes deepen understanding*

**9. Multiple External Representations / DeFT Framework** (Shaaron Ainsworth, 2006)
Learning one content through several representations deepens understanding three ways: complementing (different aspects), constraining (fewer errors), constructing (built abstraction). The representations must differ enough — near-duplicates waste cycles, alien ones break coherence.
- *Source: Learning and Instruction, 16(3), 183-198 (2006)*
- *Carries over as: independent validation of the 2–8 pass range as well-calibrated, and of the requirement that lenses differ structurally rather than merely re-running*

**10. Perspectivism** (Friedrich Nietzsche, 1887; Ronald Giere, 2006)
"The more eyes, different eyes, we train on the same matter, the more complete will our concept of it be." Giere extended the point to scientific modeling — every model is irreducibly perspectival and partial.
- *Source: On the Genealogy of Morals (1887); Scientific Perspectivism (2006)*
- *Carries over as: each pass is another "eye" — objectivity accumulates from partial views, never from a view from nowhere*

### AI/ML & Prompt Engineering

**11. Self-Consistency** (Wang et al., 2022)
Sample several diverse reasoning paths for one problem; keep the most consistent answer. Gains of +17.9% on GSM8K, +11.0% on SVAMP, +12.2% on AQuA over single-path chain-of-thought.
- *Source: arXiv:2203.11171*
- *Carries over as: multiple reasoning paths = multiple lenses on the same ISC extraction*

**12. Multi-Agent Debate** (Du et al., 2023)
Several AI agents study one problem, dispute their findings, and converge through structured disagreement on better answers.
- *Source: arXiv:2305.14325*
- *Carries over as: each pass could be a separate "agent perspective"*

**13. Ensemble Methods** (Breiman, Schapire, Freund)
Merged models outscore any single model. Bagging, boosting, and random forests all harvest diverse perspectives on shared data.
- *Carries over as: merging several ISC extraction passes = an ensemble of requirements*

**14. DiVeRSe (Diverse Verifier on Reasoning Step)** (Li et al., 2023)
Diverse prompts for one problem plus per-step verification of reasoning. Structural prompt diversity buys coverage.
- *Carries over as: structurally distinct lenses = diverse prompt engineering*

### Requirements Engineering

**15. Viewpoint-Oriented Requirements Engineering** (Finkelstein & Nuseibeh, 1992)
Elicit requirements through multiple stakeholder viewpoints, each holding partial knowledge. Disagreements between viewpoints expose hidden requirements.
- *Source: Requirements Engineering Journal; IEEE Conf on RE*
- *Carries over as: each pass adopts another stakeholder viewpoint*

**16. Misuse Cases** (Sindre & Opdahl, 2005)
Study one system through an adversary's eyes to surface security and safety requirements invisible from the user's seat.
- *Carries over as: the Failure/Adversarial lens*

**17. Progressive Elaboration** (PMBOK/PMI)
Project understanding sharpens over time. Each pass adds detail and precision to requirements that began vague.
- *Carries over as: each pass adds precision to ISC criteria*

### Design Thinking & Problem Solving

**18. Six Thinking Hats** (Edward de Bono, 1985)
Six structured perspectives (facts, emotions, risks, benefits, creativity, process) applied in sequence to one problem. Enforces systematic multi-angle examination.
- *Source: Six Thinking Hats (1985)*
- *Carries over as: the direct ancestor of our 8 lenses*

**19. Causal Layered Analysis** (Sohail Inayatullah, 1998)
Read one phenomenon at four depth layers: litany (surface data), social/structural causes, worldview/discourse, myth/metaphor (deep archetypes).
- *Source: Futures, 1998; metafuture.org*
- *Carries over as: depth that grows across passes, not just angles that differ*

**20. Soft Systems Methodology** (Peter Checkland, 1981)
Build several "root definitions" of one situation, each from another worldview. CATWOE analysis enforces systematic perspective shifts.
- *Carries over as: each pass drafts another "root definition" of ideal state*

## Not to Confuse With: CS Iterative Deepening

| Aspect | CS Iterative Deepening (IDDFS) | Iterative Depth (this technique) |
|--------|-------------------------------|----------------------------------|
| **Domain** | Graph/tree search algorithms | Problem understanding & requirements |
| **What iterates** | Depth limit of search | Perspective/angle of exploration |
| **Same path?** | Yes, revisits same nodes | No, structurally different each time |
| **Purpose** | Find optimal path in state space | Extract complete understanding |
| **Invented by** | Korf (1985) | Meta-pattern across many fields |
| **Output** | Single solution path | Enriched set of requirements/criteria |

The CS technique searches the SAME TREE deeper each round. Ours studies the SAME PROBLEM from DIFFERENT ANGLES each round. Kindred in spirit (both profit from re-examination), opposite in mechanism.

---

## Why It Works: Three Mechanisms

1. **Perspective Blindness Compensation** — Every single viewpoint has blind spots. Systematic rotation through viewpoints covers gaps no lone pass catches.

2. **Productive Non-Determinism** — Even under one lens, AI non-determinism surfaces slightly different facets per pass. Paired with structural variation, this turns from bug into feature.

3. **Progressive Pre-Understanding** — Each pass updates the analyst's "pre-understanding" (Gadamer), sensitizing the passes after it. Pass 5 notices what Pass 1 couldn't, because Passes 2–4 retrained the eye.
