# Explanatory Selection

## Done Looks Like

Only candidates offering a comparatively hard-to-vary causal account of the target phenomenon advance. A survivor says plainly what it explains, which physical mechanism does the work, why each part matters, how it could fail, and what stays unexplained.

## The Survivor Artifact

Every candidate must supply:

1. **Target:** phenomenon and observations, defined without borrowing the candidate's own vocabulary.
2. **Causal core:** a short causal chain from physical mechanism to target outcome.
3. **Necessary roles:** each core component, relation, and parameter plus the explanatory job it performs.
4. **Counterfactuals:** component-ablation and parameter-substitution effects.
5. **Rival comparison:** a simpler or otherwise serious rival, and one target observation it cannot explain.
6. **Risky prediction:** a discriminating outcome capable of demoting or falsifying the candidate.
7. **Cross-context behavior:** predictions across the relevant states, perturbations, development, failure, or recovery.
8. **Remaining residue:** what stays unexplained.

## The Gate

Subject every candidate to parameter substitution, component ablation, rival equivalence, cross-state comparison, and causal-core compression. Reject whatever only restates labels, explains report/access/attention/integration while the target lies elsewhere, leans on an unfalsifiable intrinsic property, declares an identity without causal consequences, or preserves fit through arbitrary post-hoc adjustment.

## Output

Return a ranked survivor portfolio: causal core, hard-to-vary score, stress-test outcomes, rival comparison, risky prediction, cross-context predictions, and remaining residue per survivor. Score explanatory quality apart from empirical support and apart from rhetorical appeal.
