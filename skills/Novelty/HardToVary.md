# Hard-to-Vary Explanations

## The Bar

A strong explanation does more than stay compatible with observations. It names a causal mechanism whose central parts resist casual alteration — change them and the explanation stops explaining. Hard-to-vary is comparative evidence of quality, never proof of truth or of uniqueness.

## What a Candidate Owes

Each candidate must declare the target phenomenon and the observations; a short causal chain running from physical mechanism to target; why every core component earns its place; what breaks if each part is removed, swapped, or shifted in parameter; which serious rival fails and where; and one risky observation or intervention capable of counting against it.

## Stress Tests

1. **Parameter substitution:** move a central threshold, timescale, coupling, or state variable; demand a specific changed outcome.
2. **Component ablation:** delete a proposed part; name the explanatory work and the observable consequence lost with it.
3. **Rival equivalence:** substitute a simpler rival for the causal core; point to the observation or intervention left unexplained.
4. **Cross-state comparison:** hold one account across the relevant states, failures, recoveries, or contexts.
5. **Causal-core compression:** shrink the theory to its minimal causal chain; whatever drops out without prediction loss was decoration.

## Automatic Demotions

Reject or demote: redescribing the target as access, report, attention, or integration; asserting an unexplained identity; hand-waving at complexity or information flow; stretchable metaphors; and retrofitting the theory around hostile results after the fact.

## The Ranking Rule

Order candidates by necessary mechanism, distinctive counterfactuals, discriminating risk, and low accommodation cost. Archive imaginative misses for later reuse; promote only what survives these tests.
